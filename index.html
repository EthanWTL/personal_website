About

Tianlong Wang is a second-year Ph.D. student in the RISE Lab , School of Data Science, University of Virginia, advised by Prof. Sheng Li. His research focuses on spatial reasoning and planning for video generation and LLM agents; current work targets continuous-control generation via video models.

Projects

  • Geometry-Aware Video Generation via Cognitive-Science-Guided Training

    Diagnosed geometric reasoning failures in image-to-video models through representation probing and controlled geometry interventions, showing that depth and object-level spatial information is encoded in the model but not effectively used to constrain generation; providing explicit depth, slot-based object features, and ray rotation cues produced only marginal gains over RGB-only inputs. Developed a cognitive-science-guided training paradigm inspired by infant geometric development, where motion and binocular cues progressively calibrate pictorial representations and entity-level reasoning; constructed an adaptive training engine that varies viewpoints, target motions, and obstacle positions based on model feedback, achieving improved geometric consistency under the same training budget. Currently developing entity-centric and causal generation mechanisms, including improved slot-based object abstraction to mitigate inter-object penetration and shorter-horizon causal prediction to discourage endpoint-first generation behaviors such as object rescaling.

  • Evaluating Spatial World Modeling in Video Generators via 3D Camera Trajectory Generation

    This project studies spatial world modeling in video generators through 3D camera trajectory generation. We build an indoor room-tour dataset from InteriorGS 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes, with floor plans, occupancy maps, and camera-trajectory annotations. Using this benchmark, we capture current video model's performance gap between local perception and spatial relationship abstraction.

  • ItinBench: Benchmarking Planning Across Multiple Cognitive Dimensions with Large Language Models

    Developed ItinBench, a benchmark for evaluating LLM planning across verbal and spatial reasoning. Built a real-world itinerary dataset from Yelp business data and user reviews, and designed an agent evaluation pipeline with tool use and route optimization metrics to measure reasoning quality and planning efficiency.
